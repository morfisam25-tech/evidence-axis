# EVIDENCE AXIS
## HENRY — EXECUTIVE INTELLIGENCE BRIEF

**Checked:** 7 September 2026  
**Decision focus:** Post–Henry Deal competitive positioning and buyer reference class

---

## Executive read

Henry has moved beyond the product that much of the public market still describes.

The July launch of Henry Deal added underwriting, comps, research, a proprietary property/transaction database, Excel-linked models, and buyer/lender lists around the original deck workflow. The problem is that external buyer surfaces have not moved at the same speed.

That creates two different positioning risks at once:

1. some public comparisons still describe Henry as a narrow deck/marketing product; and
2. broader brokerage platforms now use similar "end-to-end" and anti-fragmentation language, which can blur Henry's actual boundary.

The practical decision is not whether Henry needs a longer competitor list. It is whether the post-launch sales story should be rebuilt around **what Henry now owns, what it does not try to own, and how that boundary changes by buyer seat.**

---

# FINDING 01
## A live competitor page still describes pre–Henry Deal Henry

IntellCRE's current Henry comparison labels Henry a **"Legacy Marketing Tool"** and marks several capabilities as unavailable, including AI underwriting, automated market overview, comps/market data, and real-time AI parsing.

Henry's current product surface says the opposite on several of those points. Henry now documents:

- custom underwriting models in Excel;
- comps and transaction ranking;
- market research and sponsor context;
- a proprietary property and transaction database;
- buyer and lender lists; and
- deal writeups and pitch decks generated from the same underlying context.

This is not a subjective positioning disagreement. It is a buyer-visible factual drift between a live competitor comparison and Henry's current product evidence.

### Decision implication

Henry should treat post-launch comparison accuracy as a sales-enablement problem, not a brand problem.

A short evidence-backed rebuttal layer would do more than a generic "we've evolved" message. The useful format is a current capability proof sheet that sales can use when a buyer arrives with an old Henry reference class already in mind.

**What the evidence supports:** public comparison content is materially behind Henry's current product surface.  
**What it does not prove:** that Henry is unaware of the page, that the page is causing lost deals, or that buyers broadly accept IntellCRE's framing.

# FINDING 02
## Buildout now sits close enough to Henry's buyer evaluation set that the boundary matters

Buildout currently positions itself as an AI brokerage solution that runs the deal **from contact to commission** across CRM, marketing, data, transaction workflow, and back-office automation.

Henry Deal is narrower in organizational scope but deeper around the deal-preparation and deal-intelligence layer: underwriting, comps, research, proprietary firm context, Excel models, buyer/lender lists, and institutional deal outputs.

Independent and third-party buyer surfaces already place the two products near each other in the same broker-tool evaluation landscape. A current CRE Daily review names Buildout among Henry alternatives, while the REAL 2026 CRE tool directory places both inside the broker stack but assigns them different jobs.

That distinction matters because both companies now use anti-fragmentation language. Buildout says brokerages do not need more disconnected tools; Henry says the value is in replacing the steps rather than adding another isolated tool.

### The cleaner boundary

| Buyer question | Henry Deal | Buildout |
|---|---|---|
| What does this deal look like? | Underwriting, comps, research, firm context | Supporting property/data workflows |
| What should go in front of the client? | OMs, BOVs, pitch decks, lender/buyer lists | Proposals, marketing and listing workflows |
| Where does the deal live operationally? | Deal context/database tied to Henry outputs | Brokerage-wide CRM and transaction system |
| How broad is the operating scope? | Deal preparation and intelligence | Contact-to-commission brokerage workflow |

### Decision implication

Generic "end-to-end deal engine" language is now too broad to carry the distinction by itself.

Henry has a more defensible story in **deal context + institutional output**: the firm's historical deals, models, comps and market evidence become the context that produces the underwriting and client-ready work. Buildout's stronger public claim is brokerage-wide operating breadth.

The sales story should make that boundary explicit rather than forcing buyers to infer it.

**Evidence boundary:** this supports reference-class overlap, not a claim that Buildout is Henry's primary direct competitor or that shared customers use both products for the same workflow.

# FINDING 03
## Henry's new segments do not share one competitive set

Henry now presents Henry Deal across three distinct use cases:

- Investment Sales
- Debt & Capital Markets
- Sponsors & Owner-Operators

Public CRE software markets already separate tools by buyer seat and workflow. Current product evidence shows why:

- Dealpath is built around institutional acquisition pipelines, deal screening, comps, underwriting and portfolio context.
- Archer is concentrated on multifamily deal analysis, parsing, underwriting, comps and pipeline workflows.
- Clik.ai is positioned around underwriting, servicing and data operations for CRE and banking/lending institutions.
- Buildout is positioned around brokerage-wide CRM, marketing and back-office workflow.

These are not one-for-one Henry replacements. They are evidence that the reference class changes with the seat.

### Decision implication

One master competitor matrix is likely to create more noise than clarity.

The more useful structure is three sales-enablement maps:

**Investment Sales** — prove speed, deal context, comps, underwriting and client-ready output against broker workflow alternatives.  
**Debt & Capital Markets** — prove financing-package, lender-list and underwriting workflow against capital-markets/lender-oriented reference classes.  
**Sponsors & Owner-Operators** — prove underwriting, proprietary deal memory and capital-raising output against owner/investor analysis workflows.

The names on each map should be verified by segment before being promoted to direct competitors.

# WHAT I WOULD DO NEXT

## 1. Build a post-launch claim sheet

Keep it short enough for sales to use live. For each claim that external surfaces currently get wrong, pair:

**Old external frame → current Henry proof → source**

Start with underwriting, comps, market research, database/context, Excel workflow, and SOC 2 Type II.

## 2. Separate the three buyer seats

Do not force Investment Sales, Debt & Capital Markets, and Sponsors/Owner-Operators into one battlecard. The workflow boundary changes enough that the adjacent software set changes with it.

## 3. Define the Buildout boundary before buyers do

Henry does not need to claim a broader operating footprint than Buildout. It needs to make clear why a brokerage-wide operating system and a deal-context/output engine solve different problems even when both promise fewer disconnected tools.

## 4. Monitor buyer-visible surfaces after major launches

The IntellCRE and Airframe examples show a simple operational problem: product change can outrun external categorization. A small recurring surface check after major launches would catch this before stale descriptions become the default frame in sales conversations.

---

## Bottom line

Henry Deal expanded faster than the public reference class around Henry.

The strongest post-launch positioning move is therefore not "we do more now." It is to make the new boundary easy to verify:

**Henry turns a firm's deal history, models, comps and market context into the underwriting and client-ready work needed to win the deal.**

That is narrower than running an entire brokerage from first contact to commission, and broader than generating a deck. The current market is still describing Henry as both of those things depending on where a buyer looks.
