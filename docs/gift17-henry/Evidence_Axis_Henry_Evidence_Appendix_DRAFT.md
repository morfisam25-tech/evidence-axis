# EVIDENCE AXIS
## HENRY — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

**Checked:** 7 September 2026  
**Companion document:** Henry — Executive Intelligence Brief

---

## 1. Scope

This appendix supports one decision question:

**How should Henry position Henry Deal after expanding from deck production into underwriting, comps, research, proprietary firm context, Excel-linked models, and buyer/lender workflows?**

The review focuses on public evidence that a buyer, seller, competitor, analyst, or executive could verify without internal company access.

It does not attempt to infer Henry's internal awareness, win/loss data, roadmap, customer-by-customer deployment, or private competitive strategy.

---

## 2. Evidence handling

Sources are separated by provenance because each class can support different claims.

### Company sources
Henry pages can establish what Henry currently says its product does, whom it targets, and what security/product claims it publishes. They do not independently prove market share or customer outcomes.

### Competitor sources
IntellCRE and Buildout pages can establish how those companies publicly position themselves and how they describe Henry. They are not neutral assessments of comparative product quality.

### Third-party sources
Trade media, directories, and product-analysis pages can show how Henry is externally categorized or reported. Their methodology and recency vary, so they are used as buyer-surface evidence rather than as definitive product truth.

### Checked-date rule
Live pages were checked on 7 September 2026 unless a publication date is shown separately.

### Finding 02 evidence test

Three different source types point to the same strategic conclusion:

1. Buildout publicly owns a broader contact-to-commission operating-system claim.
2. Henry publicly owns a deeper deal-analysis/context/output workflow.
3. External buyer surfaces can place the two products in the same evaluation landscape.

The evidence therefore supports a **positioning boundary problem** even without proving direct one-for-one competition.

**Status:** VERIFIED WITH BOUNDARY.

### Finding 02 evidence boundary

Supported:
- reference-class overlap;
- external alternative/evaluation adjacency;
- a difference between Buildout's brokerage-wide operating scope and Henry's deal-context/output scope;
- shared enterprise-account visibility at Newmark.

Not established:
- that Newmark uses both products for the same workflow;
- switching or replacement;
- market-share movement;
- primary-competitor status.

**Source:** S9

### S18 — AI for CRE Collective 2026 directory
**Source:** https://aiforcrecollective.com/ai-tools-for-commercial-real-estate  
**Class:** Third-party industry directory  
**Published:** Aug 2026  
**Checked:** 7 Sep 2026

The directory categorizes Henry under underwriting/deal analysis and separately identifies tools such as Clik.ai for lenders/investors and other platforms by workflow.

### Finding 03 evidence test

Henry's own segment expansion is verified. The public software market also shows materially different workflows by seat.

The evidence does **not** justify calling Dealpath, Archer and Clik.ai direct Henry competitors. It does justify a different strategic conclusion:

**one flat competitor matrix would combine products optimized for different buyer jobs.**

The correct unit of competitive research is the Henry buyer segment, not the company name alone.

**Status:** VERIFIED AS SEGMENTED REFERENCE-CLASS FINDING.

### Finding 03 evidence boundary

Supported:
- Henry now markets to three distinct buyer/use-case groups;
- the adjacent software landscape differs by seat and workflow;
- sales enablement should segment reference classes before declaring direct competitors.

Not established:
- identical vendor shortlists across the three segments;
- direct substitution between Henry and every named adjacent product;
- win/loss outcomes.

# EVIDENCE BOUNDARIES USED IN THE BRIEF

The brief does not claim that:

- Henry was unaware of any public comparison;
- IntellCRE caused lost deals;
- Buildout is Henry's primary direct competitor;
- Newmark uses Henry and Buildout for the same workflow;
- Dealpath, Archer or Clik.ai are one-for-one Henry replacements;
- a third-party directory represents the full market;
- public absence of a dedicated CI team proves no internal competitive work exists.

The supported conclusion is narrower:

**Henry's product boundary changed faster than several public buyer surfaces and the adjacent CRE software reference classes around it. Post-launch competitive positioning therefore needs to be refreshed by buyer seat and anchored to verifiable current capabilities.**
