## 4. Finding 02 — PASS / promoted to final finding

### Finding
**Henry Deal now appears in the same buyer evaluation set as Buildout on independent public surfaces, but the two products claim different boundaries. Henry needs a sharper boundary than generic "end-to-end deal engine" language.**

### Source chain

A. Buildout current first-party positioning
- https://www.buildout.com/
- https://www.buildout.com/press/buildout-launches-crm-completing-the-industrys-first-ai-powered-end-to-end-deal-engine-for-cre
- https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/buildout-launches-crm-completing-the-industrys-first-ai-powered-end-to-end-deal-engine-for-cre-302709369.html

Buildout currently positions itself as:
- an AI brokerage solution from **contact to commission**
- one connected platform / one data layer
- CRM + marketing + data + back-office automation
- trusted by 50,000+ brokers
- a system intended to replace fragmented brokerage workflows

Buildout's current homepage displays Newmark among trusted brokerages.

B. Henry current first-party positioning
- https://www.henry.ai/
- https://go.henry.ai/

Henry currently positions Henry Deal around:
- underwriting
- comps
- research
- proprietary firm context/database
- buyer/lender lists
- Excel models
- OMs/BOVs/pitch decks

C. Independent evidence that buyers can encounter Buildout as a Henry alternative / adjacent evaluation option
- IntellCRE Henry alternatives page: https://intellcre.com/resources/alternatives/henry-ai-alternatives
- CRE Daily Henry review: https://www.credaily.com/reviews/henry-product-review/
- REAL 2026 CRE tool directory: https://www.real.dev/ai-tools-for-commercial-real-estate

These surfaces place Henry and Buildout in the same broker-tool / alternative landscape while distinguishing their jobs: Henry around branded deal materials / underwriting outputs and Buildout around brokerage CRM, marketing and back-office infrastructure.

D. Shared enterprise-customer signal — bounded
Independent Henry coverage names Newmark as a Henry customer:
- https://www.upstartsmedia.com/p/startup-henry-ai-real-estate-series-a
- https://susaventures.substack.com/p/announcing-our-seed-investment-in

Buildout's own current homepage displays Newmark among trusted brokerages.

### Research judgment
This is sufficient to establish **reference-class overlap**, not direct displacement. The strongest decision is positioning: both companies now use anti-fragmentation / end-to-end language, but Buildout claims a broader brokerage operating scope from first contact through commission while Henry is deeper around deal analysis, institutional context and deal outputs.

Allowed wording:
- **Independent buyer surfaces can place Henry and Buildout in the same evaluation set.**
- **Buildout owns a broader contact-to-commission platform claim; Henry's current evidence is deeper around underwriting, comps, research, firm context and deal outputs.**
- **Henry should define the boundary rather than rely on generic end-to-end language.**

Not allowed:
- Buildout is Henry's primary direct competitor.
- Newmark uses the two products for the same workflow.
- Newmark is switching vendors.
- Buildout is winning share from Henry.

## 5. Finding 03 — PASS as segmented reference-class finding

### Finding
**Henry's post-launch target expansion spans buyer seats that public CRE software markets treat as different reference classes. A single competitor matrix would mix tools that solve different jobs.**

### Henry's current segment expansion
Source:
- https://go.henry.ai/

Henry explicitly presents three use cases:
- Investment Sales
- Debt & Capital Markets
- Sponsors & Owner-Operators

### External market structure by buyer seat / workflow
Independent current directory:
- https://www.real.dev/ai-tools-for-commercial-real-estate

REAL explicitly organizes CRE tools by workflow and seat. It places:
- Henry AI in broker listings / pitches and broker stack
- Buildout in brokerage marketing / CRM / back office
- Dealpath in institutional acquisition pipeline / underwriting workflows
- Archer in multifamily underwriting and comps
- Blooma / Clik.ai in lender or underwriting workflows

Additional current product anchors:
- Dealpath AI: https://www.dealpath.com/ai/
- Archer underwriting: https://www.archer.re/underwriting
- Archer platform: https://www.archer.re/platform/platform-overview
- Clik.ai: https://clik.ai/

Current primary evidence shows materially different seats and jobs:
- Dealpath: institutional investment pipeline, deal screening, comps, underwriting and portfolio context.
- Archer: multifamily deal sourcing, parsing, underwriting, comps and pipeline; used by investors, brokers, lenders and property managers.
- Clik.ai: underwriting, servicing and data operations for CRE and banking / lending institutions.

### Research judgment
The evidence verifies the **segmentation problem**, not a claim that Dealpath, Archer or Clik.ai are direct one-for-one Henry competitors.

The useful executive decision is that competitive enablement should be segmented by the buyer seat Henry is pursuing. Investment-sales battlecards should not be reused unchanged for debt/capital-markets or sponsor/owner-operator conversations.

Allowed wording:
- **Henry's own expansion crosses buyer seats with different established software reference classes.**
- **The competitive set should be mapped by segment, not as one flat list.**

Not allowed:
- Dealpath / Archer / Clik.ai are all direct Henry competitors.
- Buyers in all three Henry segments evaluate the same vendors.
- Henry has already lost or gained share against any of these vendors.
